The Garber story

From a popcorn stand to 25 dealerships.

Guy S. Garber was born in 1884 on a sandy farm outside Lansing, poor enough that he spent winters indoors for lack of shoes. He sold popcorn at nine, newspapers as a teenager, farm implements with his father — and in 1907, at 23, went to Flint to work for Buick under William C. Durant. Three years later he came to Saginaw. When Buick left the retail business in 1912, he bought its interest, and by 1913 he was one of the original Buick Thirteen distributors, with a territory that ran to the Straits of Mackinac. More than once he rode the train to Flint with a brown paper bag full of cash so Durant could make payroll.

By 1938 only three of the thirteen remained, and when the rest were bought back into GM, Buick let Guy's stand. He held it until his death in 1965 — Michigan's first Buick distributorship, and the nation's last. His son Richard Garber Sr. took the store through the '60s, and in 1980 his grandson Richard J. Garber Jr. became president in the company's 73rd year, the youngest Buick dealer of its size in the nation.

Richard J. Garber Jr. set out to grow. Garber Nissan and Garber Ford in 1986, Gateway Financial and RightWay in 1996, the Saginaw Spirit in 2002, and rooftops across six states since. Today Garber is 25 new-vehicle dealerships and 17 brands, 2,500 people and $2 billion a year — the oldest family-owned Buick dealership in America, still owned by the family and still run from Saginaw.

1965 – Today

Three generations at the wheel.

Every leader of this company has been family, and each carried the same habit into a new era. Guy helped raise the money to build Saginaw's YMCA; his son Richard raised the money to renovate it; his grandson Richard J. Garber Jr. chaired the campaign for its latest expansion.

Our History

A century of automotive pioneering.

In 1907, most of Michigan still thought the horse and buggy was here to stay. Guy S. Garber, a 23-year-old farm-implement salesman, did not. Billy Durant — the founder of General Motors — noticed, and put him on the road selling Buicks and opening dealerships across Michigan.

He came to Saginaw for Buick in 1910, bought out the factory's retail interest in 1912, and by 1913 held the wholesale territory from Saginaw County to the Straits of Mackinac — one of the original Buick Thirteen. His work with GM lasted nearly 60 years, and when he died in 1965 his was the last Buick distributorship in America.

Where the name first went over a door: R. J. Garber Farm Implements, 414 N. Washington Avenue, Lansing.
